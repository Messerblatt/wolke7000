## Biography

His career began with a series of short-lived stints as a warehouse worker in the depots of old Berlin. Gaining a deep understanding of how customs operations function—along with the ability to assemble and dismantle loading cranes faster than the law allows—he steadily grew his influence within New Berlin’s supply chain.

During the founding phase of New Berlin, the city’s markets were notoriously unreliable, peddling cheap goods at unfair prices. Solo Mio, by contrast, stood out by offering products of the highest quality. His concept for the decentralized "meta-ship" [[ships/EVERYTHING|EVERYTHING]] allowed him to shorten supply chains and bypass middlemen; this drove down costs and enabled him to consistently undercut his competition. At the beginning of the story, his attempt to seize a monopoly on New Berlin’s logistics is still underway.

Thanks to his services as a procurer, smuggler, and integrator, the "The Real Solo Mio" seal of quality soon became established in New Berlin; it denotes goods that promise the highest quality at the lowest price.

His helmet, dating back to his time as a warehouse worker, can now be found in a display case at the Ministry of Chive Reform.
## Personality

Solo Mio is in his mid-forties, good-natured, and always up for a clandestine, late-night run—provided that a harbor awaits at the end of the journey, and someone is there in that harbor, ready with cash.

During his time working in a warehouse, he frequently listened to Luciano Pavarotti’s "’O sole mio" and mistakenly believed the song was addressing him by name. While "’O sole mio" translates roughly to "my sun," "Solo Mio" could be interpreted more like "everything for me alone." Regardless of whether it’s a misunderstanding or not, he is a "sun" that warms every room he enters—and then promptly sends an invoice for the service provided.

He embodies a blend of a Hanseatic merchant and a Mediterranean bazaar trader: he pours a drink for the person he’s dealing with, earns their trust, and then proceeds to drive the price down. He has a knack for spotting the good in people—his personal credo being: "If someone doesn't know their own worth yet, they’re hard to sell."

He is stout and robust, boisterous and jovial, and always surrounded by people—some who genuinely enjoy his terrible Luciano Pavarotti impressions, and others who are forced to endure them because they still owe him money.

When meeting new business partners, he hands them his signature "business card": a delivery note. He then promptly orders them to go ahead and deliver the goods listed on it.

An avid collector of harpoons, he haunts the shops of New Berlin, always on the lookout for new, exquisite specimens. Occasionally, he organizes expeditions to Italy to track down exceptionally rare harpoons in the contested territories. As a result, he is better informed than most about what is happening in the outer regions of New Berlin, and he frequently acts as an informant. Naturally, he charges a fee for his services—to cover his risk-related expenses.
## Skills

Solo Mio’s most important gadget is [[Michaelangelo]]—a harpoon inspired by the Sistine Chapel, featuring a stereoscopic halo-sight and a six-shot "Creation" cylinder.

In situations where [[Michaelangelo]] isn’t required, Solo Mio relies on his charm and negotiating prowess. Since he isn’t looking to make enemies—but is instead always planning his next big score—it’s unwise to cross him. After all, he wants the same thing you do: to become filthy rich and own a house made of solid gold. He’s already set a delivery date.

## Relationships

He holds Koko in high regard.

When [[Fibo]] begins one of his technical expositions, Solo Mio quickly grows bored—often clearing his throat in preparation for an impromptu musical performance.

He gets along famously with [[Spasti]]. While [[Spasti]] practices "applied Marxism" by making off with other people’s property, Solo Mio practices "applied market economy" by buying that same property from him—all while brandishing stacks of banknotes, of which he always carries far too many. No one knows where the money comes from; where it ends up, however, is usually quite clear.
